# Season 2 research: what to change from EP26 on

Researched on **1 November 2026** for the EP26 relaunch (the user asked for 60-second episodes, drastically better style and animation, and a format "based on the most popular trend patterns"). Web sources were read on that date. Tool claims marked **measured** were tested in this repo's cloud container (4 vCPU, ~15 GB RAM, no GPU) the same day.

**How much to trust this.** Almost everything published about short-form "algorithms" in 2026 comes from vendor blogs (caption apps, AI-video tools, agencies). They repeat each other's numbers, rarely cite a primary source, and often disagree. Treat percentages here as *directional*. Platform help pages are the only primary sources, and they are cited as such. Where I could not verify something, I say so.

## 1. What makes 60-second educational Shorts work in late 2026

### Hooks (first 1–3 s)
- The swipe decision is made at or before the 1-second mark, so the **first frame** has to carry the promise. Stack picture, on-screen text and the spoken line together rather than letting one follow the other ([vidIQ, 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)). Other guides still say "first 3 seconds" ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention), [ShortGenius](https://shortgenius.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices)).
- "A strong hook does not need to be loud, but it does need to be legible" ([ShortGenius](https://shortgenius.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices)). Captions visible on the first frame are widely recommended. The "+25–40 % sound-off retention" figure ([Aibrify](https://aibrify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook)) traces to no primary source. **Unverified.**
- **For us:** EP26's frame 0 already shows the predator, its herd and the question in big type. Nothing waits for the narrator, and there's no logo animation (the ≤1.5 s sting runs *on top of* the hook).

### Re-hooks and pattern interrupts (≈15 / 30 / 45 s)
- Guides recommend a visible change every 3–5 s and a small "open loop" every 10–15 s to win back drifting viewers ([Shortzly](https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies), [Edicionvideopro, TikTok](https://edicionvideopro.com/en/editing-for-platforms-video-marketing/pattern-interrupts-tiktok-retention-guide/), [Grow-it](https://www.grow-it.se/en/resources/short-form-video-scripting-retention-hooks)). The claimed "+40–60 % retention" has no published data behind it. **Unverified.**
- **For us:** EP26 changes something every 2–4 s (camera move, HUD event, stamp, card row). It re-hooks explicitly at 21 s ("Four are easy. The fifth is the twist.", with a locked fifth row left on screen as a visual open loop) and pays it off at 43 s ("Rule five? Delete the monster.").

### Loops, length and 60 s vs 30 s
- Replays count as watch time; Shorts can show more than 100 % average view duration in Studio because of loops ([Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-retention), [Postcrest](https://postcrest.com/academy/how-to-create-viral-youtube-shorts)). Ending on a frame that flows into the first is standard advice.
- Shorts have been allowed up to **3 minutes** since 15 Oct 2024 (announced by YouTube on 3 Oct 2024; full rollout of the creation tools in early 2025) ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2024/10/04/youtube-shorts-expands-video-length-to-three-minutes/), [Engadget](https://engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/youtube-shorts-can-now-run-up-to-three-minutes-160002081.html), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-3-minute-shorts-to-all-users/736967/)).
- The vendor consensus is that 30–60 s is the sweet spot and that the signal is **completion and total watch time**, not shortness. Completion falls with length (roughly 80–90 % at 15 s to 40–55 % at 60 s), but total watch time can still favour 60 s, and a "longer-short" (45–90 s) trend is reported ([SureShot](https://sureshot.video/blog/how-long-is-a-short-form-video), [SyncStudio](https://syncstudio.ai/learn/short-form-video-length-guide), [Mango](https://www.mangoagent.ai/blog/short-form-video-trends-2026)). For educational content, estimates range from 25–45 s ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention), [Aibrify](https://aibrify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook)). **No platform publishes a 60-vs-30 comparison**, and the vendor numbers conflict.
- From 24 Aug 2026, YouTube counts a Shorts view when playback starts, while monetisation stays on "engaged views" (reported in several 2026 algorithm guides, e.g. [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm-2026)). **Not checked against a YouTube primary source.**
- **For us:** 60 s only works if it earns the extra 30 s. The new format spends them on *two demonstrations* (a failed run and a fixed run), a twist, and a takeaway the viewer can save. It is not a slower version of the old shape. The last 0.7 s returns to the exact opening frame.

### Captions and on-screen text
- Word-by-word (or 2–3-word burst) animated captions are the 2026 baseline: bold, outlined, with emphasis words in an accent colour ([Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/word-by-word-captions-vs-full-sentence-captions), [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-caption-subtitle-best-practices), [Blitzcut, Shorts](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-style-youtube-shorts-2026)). "~85 % watched muted" appears everywhere without a primary source. **Unverified.** I found no evidence on ideal text density for *educational* Shorts. **Gap.**
- **For us:** S2 captions are 1–3-word bursts (≤13 characters) in Inter 900 caps with a heavy outline. Each word pops on its own timestamp. Where a kinetic title already says the words (the hook, "IT ENDS IT.", "RULE 5?", the lesson, the CTA card), the captions step aside so nothing is said twice on screen.

### Comments, shares and saves
- YouTube's own explainer lists clicks, watch time, survey responses, sharing, likes and dislikes as signals. "Sends" are not named separately. Vendor claims that external shares weigh about 5× more, or that saves equal shares, are **unverified** ([Socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/youtube-marketing/youtube-algorithm), [Kineclip](https://kineclip.com/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm-2026/)).
- **For us:** the episode ends on a concrete, screenshot-able artefact (the 5-row **Design Check** card). That gives people something to save and send. The CTA asks for a *share* to a specific person, as the plan specifies, and the pinned comment asks a question only a creator can answer ("which rule does your monster break?").

### Series and recurring formats
- Hootsuite's 2026 trends research and design-trend roundups report audiences favouring *cozy, calming* content over overstimulation ([Hootsuite](https://www.hootsuite.com/research/social-trends), [Designity](https://www.designity.com/blog/2d-animation-trends-2026)). Series with a recurring frame make each episode easier to recognise in the feed. That is creator consensus rather than a measured study.
- **For us:** one persistent world (the Isle), one recurring device (**▶ RUN**), one recurring artefact (the field card), one sting. Viewers can recognise an episode from any single frame.

### AI-content labelling (checked 1 Nov 2026)
- **YouTube** (primary source, [Help: altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)): creators must disclose *realistic* AI content (a real person saying something they didn't, altered real events or places, realistic scenes that didn't happen), and AI music when it's the main focus. Clearly unrealistic or animated content does not require disclosure. YouTube may label AI animation automatically in the expanded description. The page carries no date.
- **TikTok:** the synthetic-media policy has required labels on *realistic* AI images, audio and video since 2023. TikTok also auto-labels from C2PA metadata ([TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/19/tiktok-debuts-new-tools-and-technology-to-label-ai-content/amp/); 2026 summaries: [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance/), [Cinerads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/tiktok-ai-content-policy)). **I could not load TikTok's own help page.**
- **Meta (Instagram/Facebook):** the per-post label is "AI info" (renamed from "Made with AI"), applied on disclosure or detected metadata. Realistic AI video *or realistic AI voice-over* may require it. An account-level "AI creator" label tested in May 2026 was renamed "AI-generated profile" (around 31 Aug 2026) and targets AI *personas*: "Creators who simply use AI tools as part of their creative process don't need to add the label" ([Hypebeast, May 2026](https://hypebeast.com/2026/5/instagram-tests-voluntary-ai-creator-label-on-profiles), [Netinfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-to-limit-the-reach-of-unlabeled-ai-generated-profiles/), [Gradually.ai](https://www.gradually.ai/en/instagram-ai-label/)). **Not checked against Meta's Help Center.**
- **EU:** AI Act transparency duties apply from 2 Aug 2026 ([Postora](https://www.postora.org/blog/ai-generated-content-labels-social-media-2026)). **Secondary source only.**
- **Our policy is unchanged:** the AI label stays **on** on all platforms. EP26's world is drawn in code, but its narration is a synthetic (Kokoro) voice and the creature card art is AI-generated (Figma), and a synthetic voice is exactly what Meta names. The label costs no reach on YouTube, per YouTube's own page.

## 3. The niche: what others do, and the gap

- **Long-form essays:** *Hello Future Me* (worldbuilding analysis using ASOIAF and Avatar), *Artifexian* (science-based worldbuilding, including a speculative-biology log series; that claim's source is AI-generated and marked weak), *The Lore Lounge* (lore travelogues), *World in Progress* (one creator building one world) ([YouTube wiki: Hello Future Me](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Hello_Future_Me), [Artifexian](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Artifexian), [The Lore Lounge](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCrZ-Dfx_9ugXPUyVJnmi0FQ), [World in Progress](https://www.youtube.com/@WorldInProgressYT/videos)).
- **Maps:** *Map Men* (comedic map explainers, 1–5 M views per video) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map_Men)), plus many fantasy-map retrospectives.
- **Speculative evolution:** *The Future Is Wild*, *Snaiad*, Curious Archive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Future_Is_Wild), [Snaiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snaiad)).
- I could not get subscriber or view counts for most of these. Search didn't surface them, so I have **no ranking**.
- **The gap:** the niche either *talks* (essays, narration over art) or *shows a finished world* (maps, lore). Nobody I found **demonstrates a worldbuilding principle as a running system** in under a minute: the naive design breaking, then the fix working, in the same world, with numbers on screen. That is Season 2's angle.

## 5. Decision

**Season 2 = "THE WORLD ENGINE."** One persistent pixel-voxel diorama world (the Isle) where every episode *tests* a worldbuilding idea by pressing **▶ RUN**. The naive design visibly breaks, the fix visibly works, and a twist shows what most people miss. The viewer leaves with a field card checklist.

It combines the three strongest fits above: a miniature diorama, pixel art (low-res 3D) and a simulation. It's distinctive in the niche, reads on a phone, and extends to the remaining arcs: history (fast-forward centuries), war (supply lines), economy (trade flows), endings (what the world does after).

**Rejected:**
- a guide character: costly to animate well, and it adds a face to an AI-voiced channel;
- hand-drawn ink and paper cut-out: not convincing in code;
- a pure 2D pixel engine: less depth, and less of a "world".

Details: `docs/STYLE_S2.md`. EP26 production notes: `episodes/ep26/BRIEF.md`.

## 6. Follow ask, part numbers and the sound logo (2 Nov 2026, EP26 v3)

The user asked for the episode number to be spoken, a follow ask "in the most effective way you can research and apply", and a recurring sound like Season 1's bells, but different. Searched on 2 Nov 2026. The claims below were then re-read on the source pages the same day, because the search summaries overstated several of them (noted where).

### Where and how to ask for the follow
- **Final seconds, spoken and shown.** The most effective Shorts CTAs are "visual and auditory prompts that appear in the final 3-5 seconds of the clip": "a clear, single instruction before the video loops". A text overlay works "even when the video is viewed on mute". YouTube's interactive end screens "are not available for videos under 60 seconds", so the ask has to be in the picture ([Fluxnote, updated 22 Oct 2026](https://fluxnote.io/guides/call-to-action-youtube-shorts)).
- **The loop makes the end valuable.** "The last 5 seconds of a Short are looped most frequently, making them the most powerful real estate for a subscribe CTA." The final CTA should be "specific, benefit-focused, visual overlay plus verbal" ([Virvid, 3 Mar 2026](https://virvid.ai/blog/turning-shorts-views-into-subscribers-2026)).
- **Specific beats generic.** "The series CTA converts better than the generic follow CTA because it creates a specific reason to return." People follow "for the resolution to something specific they already care about" ([Scrollscript, 5 Apr 2026](https://scrollscript.ai/blog/video-call-to-action-examples-short-form)). The page gives no test data.
- **Mid-video asks, only after the payoff.** "A mid-video CTA can interrupt the payoff if it arrives before the central point lands"; in short-form, "the best CTA may be brief copy that appears after the value is clear" ([BiViSee, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.bivisee.com/capabilities/video-visual-marketing/video-cta-behavior-placement/)).
- **Numbers I'm not using:**
  - A search summary attributed a "+18 % subscriber gain from an animated 'Follow for Part 2' overlay" to Fluxnote. The page only suggests testing such an overlay and reports no result.
  - Virvid quotes, secondhand, "67 % higher subscriber conversion" for series formats (Swydo, 2025) and "74 % of all Shorts views come from non-subscribers" (Loopex Digital, Q1 2026).
  - **Unverified.**
- **Seen in the search, not relied on:**
  - [Viral.new](https://viral.new/blog/short-form-video-trends)
  - [Scrollscript, writing a CTA](https://scrollscript.ai/blog/how-to-write-a-cta-for-short-form-video)
  - [DriveEditor](https://driveeditor.com/blog/best-cta-formats-for-short-videos)
  - [PlayPlay](https://playplay.com/blog/video-cta-examples/)
  - [Lemonlight](https://www.lemonlight.com/blog/7-tips-for-creating-a-strong-video-cta/)
  - [Gumlet](https://www.gumlet.com/learn/video-cta-types-ranked/)
  - [Faceless](https://faceless.so/blog/video-call-to-action-tactics-that-get-clicks)

### A sound logo
- **Logo vs. signature sounds.** An audio logo is a brief, memorable sound of "roughly two to five seconds". Separately, "signature sounds are recurring audio elements used in specific contexts" (transitions, UI interactions, success moments, alerts) that "reinforce brand identity through repetition and association" ([Wubble docs](https://wubble.ai/docs/studio-tools/brand-kit/sonic-identity), undated).
- **Consistency.** "Consistency beats novelty: Your audio logo should evolve but remain recognizable", and "Most successful jingles between 3–7 seconds anchor memory faster" ([Soundverse](https://www.soundverse.ai/blog/article/audio-logo-creation-with-ai-brand-sound-guide-0855), undated). Also seen: [Massive Music, 2026](https://massivemusic.com/soundboard/why-sonic-branding-matters-more-than-ever-in-2026), on sonic branding as a system of sounds rather than one sting.
- **Limits.** All of these are vendor pages, and none studies short-form video. **I found no platform data on creator sound logos.**
- **Season 1's bells**, for contrast: inharmonic sine bells (partials 1, 2.76, 5.4, 8.93) rung over the chords of the intro and outro sections. The Season 2 chime is deliberately different. It is a sampled music box with 8-bit square blips in open fifths and a clockwork wind-up, and it's tied to *actions* (start, lesson, follow), not to sections.

### What EP26 v3 does with it
| Finding | In the episode |
|---|---|
| Ask in the final seconds, spoken and shown; one instruction before the loop | The last line: "Part 26 of 50. Follow for part 27: a dragon lands here." A pixel FOLLOW button is tapped on "Follow" (57.1 s). Captions step aside, so the button is the text |
| A specific reason to return | "For part 27", plus a locked NEXT RUN card that decodes to DRAGONS, plus a dragon's shadow crossing the Isle while the Grimmaw looks up (the open loop) |
| Mid-video ask only after the value lands, and brief | `followChip` (+ FOLLOW · PART 26/50, no voice) for about 2 s, right after "Rule five is its job." |
| Series identity | The number is spoken ("Part 26 of 50") and shown (a 50-segment season bar), the same way every episode |

| Consistency | One rendered clip, always on D and never ducked, so it sounds identical every time |

**Not knowable from here:** whether any of this lifts follows on *this* channel.
- Compare EP26's follows per 1,000 views with EP25's and EP27's, alongside the share of viewers still watching at 55 s.
- If the mid-video chip costs watch time, drop it.

## 7. Benchmarking the writing and the timeline (12–13 Nov 2026, EP31)

The user asked for EP31's writing to be benchmarked against top-performing content "from start to finish", and for EP31 to be the reference for the episodes after it. Searched on 12 Nov 2026. The pages quoted below were re-read on 13 Nov 2026, because the search summaries again overstated or mixed up several numbers.

**What "benchmarking" can mean from here.** I have no access to anyone's analytics and can't watch other channels' videos, so I could not measure top-performing Shorts directly. What I could do:
1. collect what is published about how high-retention Shorts are written and cut;
2. record how strong each source is;
3. turn the patterns that several sources agree on into **measurable targets**;
4. score EP31, and earlier episodes, against them with one script (`pipeline/benchmark.py`).

The scorecard is a consistency check that catches a slow hook or a static stretch before a render. It does not predict retention.

### What the platform itself says (primary source)
- YouTube's blog, 19 Aug 2026 ([What are 'Engaged' Views on YouTube?](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)): "Starting August 24, 2026, views across YouTube are counted from the first frame going forward." For a Short in the feed: "The moment it starts to play, it's counted as a view." "An engaged view is defined as clicking to watch or watching past the initial seconds." Also: "the vast majority of analytics remain anchored on engaged views", and there is "no change to recommendations".
- This confirms the item §1 marked as unchecked (the 24 Aug 2026 change). Shorts had already counted a view on play or replay since 31 Mar 2025 ([Gyre, 16 Jun 2026](https://gyre.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-view-count-update-impact-strategy-what-to-do-next), citing YouTube Help).
- **What follows:** a raw view says nothing about the hook. The number to watch in Studio is "viewed vs swiped away" (Gyre calls it "the strongest indicator of whether your hook is working"), then average percentage viewed.

### The first seconds
- **No platform publishes a threshold.** Retensis says so about its own tiers: "These are targets we tell creators to aim at, not measured platform averages"; "no platform publishes a cutoff". Its tiers for swipe-away in the first three seconds: "below 15% is exceptional", "15% to 25% is healthy", "above 35% is a hook that is not working"; for 30–60 s Shorts it aims for average view duration "above 40%" ([Retensis, 7 Apr 2026, updated 7 Sep 2026](https://retensis.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-explained)).
- Other vendors give different numbers for the same thing: "70%+ viewed is viral territory", 50/50 is average ([Gyre](https://gyre.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-view-count-update-impact-strategy-what-to-do-next), no source). Virvid quotes, secondhand, a study of 3.3 billion Shorts by Paddy Galloway in which "a 'Viewed vs. Swiped Away' (VVSA) ratio of 70-90% performed best" ([Virvid, 16 Feb 2026](https://virvid.ai/blog/first-3-seconds-hook-faceless-shorts-2026)). **I did not read the study itself.**
- **How the opening should be built** (Virvid, same page): "Your first spoken word should land within half a second of the video starting", with no "music intro, no logo animation"; "The opening visual should be the most striking frame in the entire video"; "Captions should appear from the very first word." It names five hook structures: bold claim, curiosity gap, micro-story opening, visual shock and direct question.
- "The first two to three seconds should be the most deliberately constructed part of the video", and the value should be "clear within the first ten seconds" ([Masterhooks](https://masterhooks.com/resources/articles/short-form-video-checklist-for-high-retention); the page shows two different dates).
- Hook length: "1-3 seconds maximum", about 15 words for a 60 s video ([Eliro, 7 Apr 2026](https://eliro.pro/blog/short-form-video-hooks-script-guide-2026)); "Hook (1–2 seconds)" ([Toptal, 2026](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-write-a-youtube-script)).

### Structure, pace and payoff
- **One idea.** A Short is "Hook (1–2 seconds)", "Context (1 short line)", "Value (core takeaway)", "CTA (optional but focused)": "Use one idea, one example, one takeaway." "Shorts reward precision. Filler is fatal." ([Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-write-a-youtube-script)).
- **Written for the ear.** "Use short sentences. Use contractions. Avoid academic phrasing." "If you wouldn't say it out loud, rewrite it." Cut "10 to 20% before filming" (Toptal).
- **Beats and timing.** "Hook > Problem > Solution > CTA": hook 0–3 s, problem 3–15 s, solution 15–45 s with scene changes "every 2-4 seconds", CTA in the final 5 seconds; 60 seconds is "140-160 words" at "about 2.5-3 words per second"; "Each beat should deliver a micro-payoff that earns the next few seconds of attention." ([Eliro](https://eliro.pro/blog/short-form-video-hooks-script-guide-2026)).
- **Tension.** A "'but therefore' problem-solution structure" gives a reason to stay, and the video should "end with something the viewer can do, feel, or think differently about" ([Masterhooks](https://masterhooks.com/resources/articles/short-form-video-checklist-for-high-retention)).
- **Promise and payoff.** A script works by "delivering on the thumbnail's promise, nothing more, nothing less"; "hold back one fact the viewer is waiting for until late in the video"; in a versus format, "Delay the verdict to 80% of the video"; "tease a specific secret in the middle to pull viewers past 50%". The page is about long-form scripts, and it calls the often-quoted lift from pattern interrupts "directional, not study-backed" ([OutlierKit, 20 Feb 2026, updated 6 Nov 2026](https://www.outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-script-writing/)).
- "Deliver on your hook's promise." "Deliver value first, ask second." (Eliro).

### Comments
- **The one large dataset in this note** ([Socialinsider, 5 Aug 2026, updated 11 Nov 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/amp/)): 36.7 million Reels, 35.6 million TikTok videos and 7.5 million Shorts from brand accounts with about 1K to 1M followers, January 2025 to June 2026. Engagement rate by followers: TikTok 2.60% (down from 3.70% in 2025), Reels 0.45%, Shorts 0.30%. Average comments per video: TikTok 50, Reels 20, Shorts 10. Two cautions: these are brand accounts, and TikTok's rate includes shares while the other two count only likes and comments.
- **A comment is expensive.** "A like takes 0.5 seconds. A comment takes 10+ seconds." (unsourced). Advice: "Place the CTA in the last 2 seconds of your video AND in the caption", "Post the first comment on your own video immediately", "Respond to all comments in the first hour after posting" ([Socialync, 25 Feb 2026](https://www.socialync.io/blog/increase-tiktok-comments-engagement-2026)).
- **The binary choice.** A prompt that "Poses a binary choice where both options have vocal defenders"; "the split-screen format itself invites a side to be taken." The limit: "The goal is friction, not offense", because "Hooks engineered purely to provoke anger tend to attract comment sections full of anger." ([Influencers Time, 14 Sep 2026](https://www.influencers-time.com/comment-bait-hooks-engineering-replies-without-rage-bait-ris/)).
- **No source measures it.** None of these pages reports a test showing that a binary prompt earns more comments than an open question. It is a reasoned guess: fewer words to type.
