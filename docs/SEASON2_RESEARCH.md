# Season 2 research: what to change from EP26 on

Researched on **1 November 2026** for the EP26 relaunch (the user asked for 60-second episodes, drastically better style and animation, and a format "based on the most popular trend patterns"). Web sources were read on that date. Tool claims marked **measured** were tested in this repo's cloud container (4 vCPU, ~15 GB RAM, no GPU) the same day.

**How much to trust this.** Almost everything published about short-form "algorithms" in 2026 comes from vendor blogs (caption apps, AI-video tools, agencies). They repeat each other's numbers, rarely cite a primary source, and often disagree. Treat percentages here as *directional*. Platform help pages are the only primary sources, and they are cited as such. Where I could not verify something, I say so.

## 1. What makes 60-second educational Shorts work in late 2026

### Hooks (first 1–3 s)
- The swipe decision is made at or before the 1-second mark, so the **first frame** has to carry the promise. Stack picture, on-screen text and the spoken line together rather than letting one follow the other ([vidIQ, 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)). Other guides still say "first 3 seconds" ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention), [ShortGenius](https://shortgenius.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices)).
- "A strong hook does not need to be loud, but it does need to be legible" ([ShortGenius](https://shortgenius.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices)). Captions visible on the first frame are widely recommended. The "+25–40 % sound-off retention" figure ([Aibrify](https://aibrify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook)) traces to no primary source. **Unverified.**
- **For us:** EP26's frame 0 already shows the predator, its herd and the question in big type. Nothing waits for the narrator, and there's no logo animation (the ≤1.5 s sting runs *on top of* the hook).

### Re-hooks and pattern interrupts (≈15 / 30 / 45 s)
- Guides recommend a visible change every 3–5 s and a small "open loop" every 10–15 s to win back drifting viewers ([Shortzly](https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies), [Edicionvideopro, TikTok](https://edicionvideopro.com/en/editing-for-platforms-video-marketing/pattern-interrupts-tiktok-retention-guide/), [Grow-it](https://www.grow-it.se/en/resources/short-form-video-scripting-retention-hooks)). The claimed "+40–60 % retention" has no published data behind it. **Unverified.**
- **For us:** EP26 changes something every 2–4 s (camera move, HUD event, stamp, card row). It re-hooks explicitly at 21 s ("Four are easy. The fifth is the twist.", with a locked fifth row left on screen as a visual open loop) and pays it off at 43 s ("Rule five? Delete the monster.").

### Loops, length and 60 s vs 30 s
- Replays count as watch time; Shorts can show more than 100 % average view duration in Studio because of loops ([Prepublish](https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-retention), [Postcrest](https://postcrest.com/academy/how-to-create-viral-youtube-shorts)). Ending on a frame that flows into the first is standard advice.
- Shorts have been allowed up to **3 minutes** since 15 Oct 2024 (announced by YouTube on 3 Oct 2024; full rollout of the creation tools in early 2025) ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2024/10/04/youtube-shorts-expands-video-length-to-three-minutes/), [Engadget](https://engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/youtube-shorts-can-now-run-up-to-three-minutes-160002081.html), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-3-minute-shorts-to-all-users/736967/)).
- The vendor consensus is that 30–60 s is the sweet spot and that the signal is **completion and total watch time**, not shortness. Completion falls with length (roughly 80–90 % at 15 s to 40–55 % at 60 s), but total watch time can still favour 60 s, and a "longer-short" (45–90 s) trend is reported ([SureShot](https://sureshot.video/blog/how-long-is-a-short-form-video), [SyncStudio](https://syncstudio.ai/learn/short-form-video-length-guide), [Mango](https://www.mangoagent.ai/blog/short-form-video-trends-2026)). For educational content, estimates range from 25–45 s ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention), [Aibrify](https://aibrify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook)). **No platform publishes a 60-vs-30 comparison**, and the vendor numbers conflict.
- From 24 Aug 2026, YouTube counts a Shorts view when playback starts, while monetisation stays on "engaged views" (reported in several 2026 algorithm guides, e.g. [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm-2026)). **Not checked against a YouTube primary source.**
- **For us:** 60 s only works if it earns the extra 30 s. The new format spends them on *two demonstrations* (a failed run and a fixed run), a twist, and a takeaway the viewer can save. It is not a slower version of the old shape. The last 0.7 s returns to the exact opening frame.

### Captions and on-screen text
- Word-by-word (or 2–3-word burst) animated captions are the 2026 baseline: bold, outlined, with emphasis words in an accent colour ([Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/word-by-word-captions-vs-full-sentence-captions), [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-caption-subtitle-best-practices), [Blitzcut, Shorts](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-caption-style-youtube-shorts-2026)). "~85 % watched muted" appears everywhere without a primary source. **Unverified.** I found no evidence on ideal text density for *educational* Shorts. **Gap.**
- **For us:** S2 captions are 1–3-word bursts (≤13 characters) in Inter 900 caps with a heavy outline. Each word pops on its own timestamp. Where a kinetic title already says the words (the hook, "IT ENDS IT.", "RULE 5?", the lesson, the CTA card), the captions step aside so nothing is said twice on screen.

### Comments, shares and saves
- YouTube's own explainer lists clicks, watch time, survey responses, sharing, likes and dislikes as signals. "Sends" are not named separately. Vendor claims that external shares weigh about 5× more, or that saves equal shares, are **unverified** ([Socialpilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/youtube-marketing/youtube-algorithm), [Kineclip](https://kineclip.com/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm-2026/)).
- **For us:** the episode ends on a concrete, screenshot-able artefact (the 5-row **Design Check** card). That gives people something to save and send. The CTA asks for a *share* to a specific person, as the plan specifies, and the pinned comment asks a question only a creator can answer ("which rule does your monster break?").

### Series and recurring formats
- Hootsuite's 2026 trends research and design-trend roundups report audiences favouring *cozy, calming* content over overstimulation ([Hootsuite](https://www.hootsuite.com/research/social-trends), [Designity](https://www.designity.com/blog/2d-animation-trends-2026)). Series with a recurring frame make each episode easier to recognise in the feed. That is creator consensus rather than a measured study.
- **For us:** one persistent world (the Isle), one recurring device (**▶ RUN**), one recurring artefact (the field card), one sting. Viewers can recognise an episode from any single frame.

### AI-content labelling (checked 1 Nov 2026)
- **YouTube** (primary source, [Help: altered or synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)): creators must disclose *realistic* AI content (a real person saying something they didn't, altered real events or places, realistic scenes that didn't happen), and AI music when it's the main focus. Clearly unrealistic or animated content does not require disclosure. YouTube may label AI animation automatically in the expanded description. The page carries no date.
- **TikTok:** the synthetic-media policy has required labels on *realistic* AI images, audio and video since 2023. TikTok also auto-labels from C2PA metadata ([TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/19/tiktok-debuts-new-tools-and-technology-to-label-ai-content/amp/); 2026 summaries: [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/tiktoks-2026-ai-labeling-rules-and-what-they-signal-for-platform-governance/), [Cinerads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/tiktok-ai-content-policy)). **I could not load TikTok's own help page.**
- **Meta (Instagram/Facebook):** the per-post label is "AI info" (renamed from "Made with AI"), applied on disclosure or detected metadata. Realistic AI video *or realistic AI voice-over* may require it. An account-level "AI creator" label tested in May 2026 was renamed "AI-generated profile" (around 31 Aug 2026) and targets AI *personas*: "Creators who simply use AI tools as part of their creative process don't need to add the label" ([Hypebeast, May 2026](https://hypebeast.com/2026/5/instagram-tests-voluntary-ai-creator-label-on-profiles), [Netinfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-to-limit-the-reach-of-unlabeled-ai-generated-profiles/), [Gradually.ai](https://www.gradually.ai/en/instagram-ai-label/)). **Not checked against Meta's Help Center.**
- **EU:** AI Act transparency duties apply from 2 Aug 2026 ([Postora](https://www.postora.org/blog/ai-generated-content-labels-social-media-2026)). **Secondary source only.**
- **Our policy is unchanged:** the AI label stays **on** on all platforms. EP26's world is drawn in code, but its narration is a synthetic (Kokoro) voice and the creature card art is AI-generated (Figma), and a synthetic voice is exactly what Meta names. The label costs no reach on YouTube, per YouTube's own page.

## 3. The niche: what others do, and the gap

- **Long-form essays:** *Hello Future Me* (worldbuilding analysis using ASOIAF and Avatar), *Artifexian* (science-based worldbuilding, including a speculative-biology log series; that claim's source is AI-generated and marked weak), *The Lore Lounge* (lore travelogues), *World in Progress* (one creator building one world) ([YouTube wiki: Hello Future Me](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Hello_Future_Me), [Artifexian](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Artifexian), [The Lore Lounge](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCrZ-Dfx_9ugXPUyVJnmi0FQ), [World in Progress](https://www.youtube.com/@WorldInProgressYT/videos)).
- **Maps:** *Map Men* (comedic map explainers, 1–5 M views per video) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map_Men)), plus many fantasy-map retrospectives.
- **Speculative evolution:** *The Future Is Wild*, *Snaiad*, Curious Archive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Future_Is_Wild), [Snaiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snaiad)).
- I could not get subscriber or view counts for most of these. Search didn't surface them, so I have **no ranking**.
- **The gap:** the niche either *talks* (essays, narration over art) or *shows a finished world* (maps, lore). Nobody I found **demonstrates a worldbuilding principle as a running system** in under a minute: the naive design breaking, then the fix working, in the same world, with numbers on screen. That is Season 2's angle.

## 4. Tools available here (free or approved), with what was measured

| Tool | Result |
|---|---|
| **three.js + React Three Fiber via `@remotion/three` 4.0.536** | **Chosen.** **Measured:** `--gl=angle` (the series default) **cannot create a WebGL context** in this headless shell; neither can `angle-egl`, `swiftshader` or `vulkan`. **`--gl=swangle` works.** 60 frames of the voxel island rendered in 24 s including bundling. One full-size still takes about 2 s. Full-episode render time is in EP26's brief. The 2D-only EP01–EP25 keep `angle` from `remotion.config.ts`; only Season 2 renders pass `--gl=swangle`. |
| **Pixel look** | Render the WebGL canvas at ⅓ size (360×640) and scale ×3 with `image-rendering: pixelated`. This gives crisp 3-px "pixels" and roughly 9× fewer pixels to shade, which is why software WebGL is fast enough. |
| **Blender** | Not installable from apt in this container (no candidate). `bpy` wheels are hundreds of MB and add a second toolchain. Benchmarks for CPU-only headless Cycles were anecdotal (about 7 s per frame for a default cube; [Arch forum](https://bbs.archlinux.org/viewtopic.php?id=283658)). **Rejected for now:** a 60 s episode would be hours per render, and three.js inside Remotion keeps cues, captions and 3D in one deterministic timeline. |
| **Procedural pixel pipeline** | New `pipeline/pixelate.py`: re-grids generated "pixel art" onto a true pixel grid (mode-sampling, median-cut palette, hue-aware background key). |
| **Lottie** | Not needed: every overlay is React, and Lottie would add an After Effects dependency. |
| **Figma `generate_image`** | Four models listed: `gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image`, `gpt-image-2.5-flare` (default), `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst`, `gemini-3.1-flash-image`. I tried to compare three on an identical prompt, but the session's permission classifier **blocked two of the three calls** as paid transactions, so the comparison is incomplete. `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` produced an excellent, original, clean-silhouette pixel sprite on the first try. Its fine dithering re-grids cleanly at 168 px wide. **1 generation used.** |
| **Local SDXL-Lightning** (`pipeline/local_stills.py`) | Still available for free plates. Not used in EP26, because the Isle is drawn in code. |
| **Narration** | Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) stays. VOICES.md grades `af_heart` as target A (overall grade not listed), `af_bella` A−, `af_nicole` and `bf_emma` B−; male voices C+ or lower ([VOICES.md](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md)). The alternatives: Chatterbox / Chatterbox-Turbo is MIT but a vendor CPU benchmark measured about 48 s to first audio and 7.5 GB peak memory, which is impractical here. Kitten TTS (Apache-2.0) was slower than Kokoro in the same benchmark and is lower quality. Piper's licence is listed inconsistently (MIT vs GPL-3.0) ([Picovoice benchmark](https://picovoice.ai/blog/on-device-tts/), [Speakeasy](https://www.tryspeakeasy.io/blog/open-source-text-to-speech-2026), [LocalAIMaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/kokoro-vs-xtts-vs-chatterbox)). **Kept:** `af_heart` at speed **1.05** for a brisker host read, with continuity of voice across the relaunch. |
| **Music generators** | MusicGen weights are CC BY-NC (no commercial use); YuE2 weights are CC BY-NC; Stable Audio Open's licence is reported inconsistently. ACE-Step 1.5 (MIT or Apache, reported both ways) and HeartMuLa (Apache-2.0) are permissive but need a GPU with 6–8 GB to be practical ([Boppy, licences](https://boppy.me/blog/open-source-ai-music-model-licenses), [Dev.to ACE-Step guide](https://dev.to/czmilo/ace-step-15-the-complete-2026-guide-to-open-source-ai-music-generation-522e)). **Rejected for CPU.** EP26 v1 synthesised its score in `pipeline/score_s2.py`; **EP26 v2 (2 Nov 2026) plays a composed score on sampled instruments** (FluidSynth + the MuseScore General SoundFont). See the note below the table. |

**Music update (2 Nov 2026).** After seeing v1 the user felt the music should be *real music*. EP26 v2 replaces the synthesised beat grid with a **composed score on sampled instruments**:
- FluidSynth 2.3.4 with the **MuseScore General** SoundFont (MIT, parts public domain or CC0; Debian package `musescore-general-soundfont-small`), arranged in `pipeline/music_s2.py`;
- deterministic, timed to the picture, free, and licence-clean.

Not used:
- GeneralUser GS: GitHub downloads are blocked by this session's network policy.
- ACE-Step 1.5: its official install guide has a CPU-only mode but publishes no CPU timings, and it needs about 10 GB of models.
- Stable Audio Open Small: gated on Hugging Face.

## 5. Decision

**Season 2 = "THE WORLD ENGINE."** One persistent pixel-voxel diorama world (the Isle) where every episode *tests* a worldbuilding idea by pressing **▶ RUN**. The naive design visibly breaks, the fix visibly works, and a twist shows what most people miss. The viewer leaves with a field card checklist.

It combines the three strongest fits above: a miniature diorama, pixel art (low-res 3D) and a simulation. It's distinctive in the niche, reads on a phone, and extends to the remaining arcs: history (fast-forward centuries), war (supply lines), economy (trade flows), endings (what the world does after).

**Rejected:**
- a guide character: costly to animate well, and it adds a face to an AI-voiced channel;
- hand-drawn ink and paper cut-out: not convincing in code;
- a pure 2D pixel engine: less depth, and less of a "world".
