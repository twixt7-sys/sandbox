# Worldcraft Shorts: series style guide

Every episode, from 01 to 50, follows this guide so the series reads as one show. It records what EP01–EP03 actually use. Change it only on purpose, and then update earlier episodes or note the break here.

## Format
- 1080×1920, 30 fps, about 30 s (27–33 s passes QA). H.264 yuv420p BT.709 + AAC, −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dB.
- 6–8 shots. Every shot carries new information; no motion for its own sake.

## Story shape (every episode)
1. **Hook, 0–4 s:** the strongest line over the most striking image. No intro, greeting or logo animation; the corner logo is already there.
2. **Example:** one original fantasy scenario, shown as cause and effect over 2–4 shots, each with a tag naming what it demonstrates.
3. **Lesson:** a clean diagram or stack on a dark background (`nightSky` scene or dimmed `plateBands`). Spell the principle out in on-screen words.
   EP09 varied it again: the hook's wrong image (desert robes in a blizzard) was struck out with `reverseCard`, then the culture was rebuilt layer by layer in cause → effect lines ("Wind bites, so…"), annotated with `callout`s and counted by `reasonTally`; the lesson and CTA return to the hook's framing with the person the climate designed, and the loop lands back on the wrong image.
   EP07 varied it again: the example was surveyed on the plate itself (the empty valley), a running `reasonTally` counted the reasons, and the overlap dissolved into the finished city.
   EP08 varied it again as a pull-back: one object (a cup of water) first, then each shot steps out a scale (house → street → road → region map), with `pullBack` shrinking the previous picture into its place in the next one. The lesson returned to the opening object.
   EP10 varied it again: the hook shows the obvious answer (the palace), the map zooms in on it and then pulls back until the real key (one bridge) appears, and the lesson sits over the consequence (the gate shut at night).
   EP11 varied it again (first non-geography episode): the same plate read twice. The visitor's read in a cold grade with a rust verdict, then `plateLook` wipes the warm insider grade across the identical frame on "Look again", and amber callouts name what the moment means. The lesson peels from the dismissed surface (the empty costume) down to values.
   EP12 varied it again as a then/now mirror: two pairs of matched plates (same frame, same objects, three centuries apart). The festival dissolves into the punishment it grew from (`eraShift`), an `originLedger` pairs each custom with its forgotten purpose, and on the lesson the festival grows back over the origin. Darkness is shown only through absence (`headCount`'s empty outlines), never harm.
   EP13 varied it again with a **split frame**: the hook plays the same gift on both sides of a border at once (top half one culture, bottom half the other, each lit as it is named, a rust INSULT stamp on each), and the collision is replayed as a split of one plate, framed on each person, showing what each side heard. The lesson is a `mirrorLedger`: rule and reason in matching columns. Split shots turn captions off (the half labels carry the line) and keep each half's label block clear of faces.
   EP14 varied it again with a **verdict, re-read**: the hook re-captions one person on one plate ("Hero." struck out, "Traitor." in rust, via `callout` `then`), and a `verdictScale` (FREEDOM / DUTY) carries the example across two plates, tipping one way for one people and the other way for the next, then comes level on "both are right about something". Morality episodes keep both sides sympathetic: no villain, consequences shown through objects (the horn left on the post), never violence.
   EP15 varied it again with an **evidence chain on the plate**: one everyday habit shown closely (quarrelling neighbours share one bench in the street until they agree), then each inference is inked onto the same plate (`inferChain`: loop the clue → what we see → ∴ the value in an ember seal), never stated as a value in narration first. The spine pulls back (`pullBack`) from the bench into the whole ordinary street, where the three values stay pinned as seals, so the plain street now reads as a civilization. No palace or ruler appears anywhere.
   EP16 varied it again (opening Arc 4) by **following the essential thing out of the palace**: the hook shows the throne and questions it, the `atlas` map traces the grain roads into one institution (the Granary Guild), one `inferChain` reads the holder's keys, and the turn is the crown itself flying from the palace to the guild while a dashed outline stays on the throne. The lesson splits one crown into TITLE ≠ POWER (`titlePower`). Power episodes name an institution or person, not a place (EP10 already did geography), and show leverage through objects, never threats.
   EP17 varied it again (Arc 4's second episode) with a **pillar stack**: the empire is drawn as a roof ("THE EMPIRE" on an entablature) on five plain stone pillars, then we meet each supporter on their own plate, numbered as a pillar (`pillarTag`: PILLAR I · BREAD / *The farmer*). On the lesson the pillars return as the people themselves (`pillarStack` portraits, each with its reason), and the last pillar is an empty dashed outline: NOTHING BETTER. Oppressive powers stay clearly oppressive; fear and force are implied (a knock at night), never shown, and no real regime's iconography (flags, salutes, uniforms, symbols) appears.
   EP18 varied it again (Arc 4's third episode) with **a law, read twice**: the law is shown first as absurd (a man fined for saying nothing), then written out on a code-drawn card (`lawText`: THE LAW OF ANSWERS, three short clauses), then the story cuts to the remembered disaster it answers (a night street where every shutter lights up, `glints`: everyone saw, nobody spoke), and on the return each clause is **lit with the fear behind it**. The lesson lifts the card ("Every strange law is a scar. *Find the wound.*"). Law text is always drawn in code, never generated; no real legal system, courtroom iconography (gavels, wigs, scales) or punishment appears, only a fine.
   EP19 varied it again (Arc 4's fourth episode) with **a feedback loop**: the empire at its height denies the cliché ("No villain required."), then each strain gets one plate and a numbered crack tag (`crackTag`: CRACK I · FAMINE … CRACK IV · OVERREACH), each line starting "So…" so the chain gathers speed. The turn joins them: the four plates become medallions on a ring round the empire's seal (`crackLoop`), the last arrow closes back to the first in rust, a pulse runs round faster each lap and every pass cracks the seal, until one ordinary event (a death with two heirs) splits it in two. Collapse is shown as process, never as violence: an empty granary, a shut gate, an army walking away, a seal breaking.
   EP20 varied it again (closing Arc 4) with **two chronicles, each with its frame**: one war remembered by two invented kingdoms. Each side's memorial is a carved `tablet` (text in code) inside a gilt `chronicleFrame` that darkens the rest of the plate; the tablet is stamped TRUE, then on "Look what it leaves out" the frame opens and a rust callout names what it cropped (the other side's dead, the other side's burned town). The final shot puts both tablets, both TRUE, over the opening valley with a stone on each bank, and `truthRows` give the reasons (losses, stories, values) before "Neither is evil." Two-nation episodes give each side a true grievance and a real loss, show loss only through memorials, cairns and ruins (never battle), and never pin the war on one side.
   EP21 varied it again (opening Arc 5) with a **form filled in**: a blank spell page drawn in code (parchment and ink, the series' only light card) shows three empty numbered lines on the hook, then rides in the top-left corner while each question is asked and its answer inked on a plate of its own (the mend works on a jar, fails on a stranger's wheel, and its price cracks the mender's window). The three answers then generate a plot on two plates (the crown shatters; the only mender who ever held it is a thief) with a `storyQuestions` chip per answer, and on the lesson the full page returns and its sigil ignites only once all three rows are written. Magic episodes show the effect in code over plates of the world and the caster (never painted spells), with one signature sound per effect (`mendKnit`), and keep each framework question light: the deeper dives (cheap magic, creative costs, technology, weaknesses) belong to EP22–EP25.
   EP22 varied it again (Arc 5's second episode) with **ripples from one cheap power**: the healing is a code-drawn jade glow under a mender's hand (`mendGlow`, motif colour mend `#a6e3c4`), and its consequences spread as rings from one plaza on a city seen from above. Each knock-on change gets its own plate, numbered as a ring (`ringTag`: RING II · WAR / *Nobody goes home*), and a faint ring sweeps across each plate as we arrive. The turn is the last ring: a dashed rust "?" that finds the one the magic couldn't save, shown through absence (the glow gutters out over a mender's empty hands beside an empty bed). The lesson lays all the rings out at once on the dimmed map. Magic episodes show consequences through people and institutions, never wounds: unhurt soldiers, an abandoned workshop, a queue.
   EP23 varied it again (Arc 5's third episode) with **a ledger, struck one line per spell**: the caster's memories are written on a dark gilt card in code (`memoryLedger`: four lines, the last her own name), which rides in the corner over each spell's plate. Each spell (a code-drawn rose glow, motif colour memory `#f3b9c8`, `mendGlow`) strikes one line in rust while the plate shows what it took: a name fading from a door (`fadingName`), a sister re-captioned "A stranger" (`callout` `then`). The turn is the last line breathing ("Worth it?"), and the lesson lifts the ledger above a row of other prices (YEARS · WARMTH · A SENSE · A MEMORY). Costs of magic are shown through what they take, never through injury or illness on screen: a fever is broken behind a closed door, and a forgotten sister is still smiling.
   EP25 varied it again (closing Arc 5) with **a weakness played as a puzzle**: one terrifying caster (the Unmaker, who erases anything she can see) shown once on plates of the world, her sight a code-drawn lilac beam (`gazeBeam`, motif colour gaze `#c7b8f5`) and the one thing she erases a gate that dissolves into the dark (`unmake`). The weakness (only what she can see) is then cut between ground plates and a top-down courtyard plan (`sightPlan`) whose sight cone is ray-cast against the cover every frame, so the blind spots are real geometry: the heroes add smoke, fog and a wall on three spoken words and a route draws through the shadows to her door. The turn shows the puzzle cutting both ways (a mirror bends her sight; lanterns; height), and the lesson lifts the solved plan with a one-line arc nod (CAN · CAN'T · COSTS · CONSEQUENCES · WEAKNESS). Weakness episodes keep the threat implied: the power touches things (a gate), never people, and the heroes are tokens or crouched in shadow.
   EP06 varied this: the lesson was the map's own key (`atlas` scene), lit row by row on the same map the example built. Maps are the default way to explain any geography mechanism in Arc 2.
4. **CTA:** return to the opening image in a changed state (EP02: the sun returns; EP03: the same reservoir at dusk), show `ctaCard`, and cross-fade the last 0.6 s into the first frame so the video loops.

## Look
- **Palette** (`series/series.json`): night `#0b1216`, slate `#16222a`, fog `#9fb1b8`, mist `#d6dfe0`, parchment `#efe6d2` (type), amber `#e9a94b` (accents, emphasis), ember `#f4c46e` (payoff words), rust `#b9543a` ("don't" or struck-out). Diagram backgrounds use deep midnight blue (`#070e1e`–`#0d1a33`). An episode may add one motif colour for its magic or resource (EP02: sun gold; EP03: water `#7fd3ec`). Name it in the episode's storyboard and keep everything else on the series palette.
- **Type:** Cormorant Garamond (display; amber or ember italic for the key words) and Inter (captions 800 weight, and uppercase kickers with 0.22–0.24em letter-spacing). Self-hosted, so never load fonts from a CDN.
- **Stills:** Figma `generate_image` (`gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image`, 1080×1920 requested), lanczos-upscaled. Every prompt starts "Vertical 9:16 cinematic fantasy documentary still" and ends "Painterly realism, atmospheric perspective, 35mm anamorphic look, soft film grain, original architecture/clothing. No text, no letters, no signage, no logos, no watermark." Figures at a modest distance with natural hands; faces partly shaded. When Figma credits are out, `pipeline/local_stills.py` (SDXL-Lightning, free) is the fallback: keep `sdxlPrompt` short (CLIP stops at 77 tokens), render 2–3 seeds, and draw in code what it won't paint (EP11's shrouded body). Invented peoples wear invented dress: if a plate reads as a specific real culture (EP09's first migration plate looked like a real reindeer-herding people, down to the tents and trim), re-roll it with more invented specifics (colours, animals, objects) rather than ship it. Keep `sdxlPrompt` to about 50 words, since the script appends a style tail and anything past CLIP's 77 tokens is dropped; ask for "a living" person, or SDXL may paint a gilded statue on a throne (EP16). SDXL also paints stray gibberish lettering (EP15's shop sign): paint it out of the plate before use, since no plate may carry readable text.
- **Grade:** the series `Grade`, `Vignette` and `Grain` on every frame (automatic in `src/Episode.tsx`). Use `plateScrim` (0.3–0.75) where type sits over a bright sky.

## Layout (px, 1080×1920 frame)
- Corner logo: x 90, y 236, 66 tall (`LogoBug`, automatic). Never cover it, and don't put the full logo anywhere except the CTA card (`hideLogoBug: true` on that shot).
- Top-left tags (`tag`): y ≥ 336. Kicker in amber caps, then one serif italic line.
- Captions: centred on y 1250, max 20 characters per line, amber on emphasis words only. Keep faces out of y 1200–1300 (use `plateFrame` to reframe).
- Keep the right edge (x > 920) and the bottom (y > 1360) free of anything important; platform buttons and captions sit there.
- Diagram block: y 340–1150.

## Motion
- Camera: slow `push`/`pull`/`drift`/`descend` only (≤ 12 % scale over a shot). No spins, shakes, whip-pans or glitch effects.
  Exception (EP10): a map may pull back from a close-up (up to 2.5×) to the whole map once, on a spoken cue like "Pull back", when the reveal *is* the story. Use `atlas` `zoom.at` so the move lands on the word and takes about 2 s.
- Magic and effects are drawn in code over the plate and pinned to it (`"local": true`), so they move with the camera.
- Text enters with a soft rise and fade or a mask reveal (≤ 0.6 s) and leaves with a short fade. One idea on screen at a time.
- Transitions: hard cut on a spoken word by default. Use dissolves (0.35–0.6 s) into diagrams and the CTA, and `fog`/`flash` only when the story motivates them.

## Sound
- Voice: Kokoro `af_heart` at speed 1.0 (or ElevenLabs when a key exists). About 70–78 words. Warm and measured, never trailer-style.
- Music: synthesized pads in `pipeline/score.py`, one key per episode, building through the example and resolving (minor → major) on the lesson and CTA. Fades out before the last frame.
- SFX: synthesized only, never sampled or downloaded. One signature sound per magic or effect, reused every time it appears.
- Mix: `pipeline/mix.py` (voice −18, music ducked about 19 LU under it). Use `music.events` ducks to let a key line land.

## CTA card
`ctaCard`: full logo (y 300) → "Episode NN / 50" kicker → episode title in italic → "YOUR TURN" (or a fitting kicker) → CTA in 3 short lines of large serif (one line in ember italic) → amber pill (y 1180).

## Delivery (every episode)
- Run `python pipeline/qa.py out/epNN.mp4 --sheet out/epNN_sheet.jpg`, inspect the sheet plus extra frames at every graphic beat, and fix and re-render before delivering.
- Hand the user both `epNN.mp4` (master) and `epNN_compat.mp4` (H.264 Main, CRF 20, `+faststart`) with `SendUserFile`, plus a private preview Artifact page. The video goes in the asset store (`capabilities: {assets: {}}`, upload with `asset: true`, then `src="/_blob/<id>"`), never as a plain page file.
- `duskGrade` always draws a reflecting pool. When the plate has no water there (EP10's plaza), move it off-frame (`pool: [540, 2400, 10, 5]`).
- Encode the player and download copies with the master's audio copied (`-c:a copy`). Re-encoding AAC at 128k pushed EP07's true peak to 0.0 dB and failed QA.
- For a then/now pair, write both prompts with the same layout sentence for sentence (EP12: wall across the middle, one arched gate centred, two round towers, open snow in the lower half); the gates landed within about 60 px, close enough for a dissolve.
- No public GitHub Release unless the user says yes for that episode (they said no for EP03).
- When SDXL won't paint a second copy of something (EP20: two standing stones, one on each bank), composite it from the plate itself: cut the object with a luminance mask, flip and scale it for distance, haze it toward the background tone and add a faint reflection. Only re-use the plate's own pixels; never paste in outside imagery.
- `pipeline/build.sh` copies `episode.json` into `public/epNN/`; after regenerating the JSON without a rebuild, copy it again (`cp episodes/epNN/episode.json public/epNN/`) or Remotion renders the old cues.
- Kokoro reads short, two-beat sentences quickly (EP23: 77 words measured 27.3 s). Bring a short script into the 27–33 s window with `pauseAfter` where a beat should land (0.6–1.1 s) and a longer `timing.tail` under the CTA card, not with filler words.
