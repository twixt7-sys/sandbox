# EP29 production brief: Immortality Would Break Your Society

**Series:** Worldcraft Shorts, Episode 29 / 50. Season 2, "The World Engine", **Arc 6: Creatures and Species** (fourth episode).

**Plan entry** (`docs/SERIES_PLAN.md`):
- Hook: "What happens when a ruler can live for ten thousand years?"
- Topic: lifespan and social structures.
- Core: long lifespans transform inheritance, education, marriage, political turnover, population growth and the preservation of knowledge. Immortality is a societal change, not just a character trait.
- CTA (a comment CTA): "Would you want an immortal ruler? Explain why."

**Deliverable:** a 61.31 s vertical Short (1080×1920, 30 fps) for YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Facebook Reels, plus Instagram Reels captions. The AI disclosure is on everywhere.

**Shot list and timings:** [`STORYBOARD.md`](STORYBOARD.md). The script that writes `episode.json`, including the whole world timeline, is `make_ep29.py`.

## The brief

The request: produce EP29 following EP26–EP28's patterns, style and vibe; close EP28's open loop ("someone on the Isle never dies"); follow EP28's §11 hand-off; end on the ending formula with a tease for EP30 ("Make a Monster That People Worship"). The standing direction from the EP27 review still holds: each episode must be **its own** at EP26's level (`docs/STYLE_S2.md` → "Make every episode its own").

### EP29's own idea: the lineage strip

A **generations timeline under the world**. On its left, the Queen's cell never changes: her portrait, AGE 30, FOREVER. On its right, one cell per generation scrolls past as the clock races: each generation is born, grows old (its hair greys), and is gone, 25 years apart, 70-year lives. Above it, in the world, the same thing happens to the people: every person in the crowd lives on the episode's clock, so a time-lapse visibly turns the valley over while one violet figure stands still. Every rule the episode switches on shows up in the strip as a badge on every generation. The episode's argument is in the device: *immortality isn't one character living long; it's every generation around her living differently.*

The strip, beat by beat:
- **Lazy version:** rust ✗ under every generation that died waiting (heirs never crowned).
- **Inheritance:** a house under every generation at fifty.
- **Power:** a council seat every fourth generation (a century).
- **Titles:** violet crowns on every generation, then only on the two youngest.
- **The twist (memory):** a lamp per generation, lit only for those alive at year zero. They're long gone, so only the Queen's lamp ("REMEMBERS") is lit.
- **The payoff:** the library lights every lamp ("NOT ALONE").

**What else is EP29's own:**
- **Camera language: a locked-off tripod.** Every tableau holds one frame (no drift, no push) while decades and centuries pass through it: seasons flicker, people are born, grey and fade, houses rise. EP26 orbited, EP27 rode a dragon, EP28 sat at the species' eye height. The only camera move in the episode is the lesson's slow wide drift.
- **Motif colour:** ageless violet `#b48cff` (the strip, the Queen's robe and banners, royal tunics, MOVING ON).
- **Music band** around the series theme:
  - `reign`: a church-organ chorale, cello pedal, timpani processional and the theme on horns, for the one who never changes (the hook and the twist);
  - `clockwork`: a harpsichord ostinato, a woodblock tick-tock, a pizzicato bass and the theme on celesta, for centuries racing past; it gains one family per fix;
  - run 1 is `drive` in the harpsichord colour;
  - the tempo is 96 BPM.
- **Sound:** `clockTicks` (a pendulum clock speeding up under each time-lapse) and `crownPass` (a generation or a crown passing).
- **Fresh wording for the recurring beats:**
  - the drop: "Forty heirs. Not one coronation.", stamped 0 CORONATIONS · 40 HEIRS WAITED;
  - the thesis: FOREVER BREAKS / EVERY RULE / BUILT FOR DEATH;
  - the checklist: UNDYING · WHAT MUST CHANGE / BUILT FOR FOREVER;
  - run 2's verdict: MOVING ON · 10 CROWNS · 40 GENERATIONS;
  - the twist: HISTORY = WHAT SHE SAYS, then THE RECORD · SHE CAN'T REWRITE IT.

**Why these fixes and this twist:** the plan lists inheritance, education, marriage, political turnover, population and knowledge. Five of the six are on screen:
- inheritance;
- turnover (POWER);
- marriage;
- population, through titles: an immortal's descendants multiply into a crowd of princes;
- knowledge, as the twist.

Education is left to the description and the comments. Memory is the twist because it's the least obvious consequence and the most dangerous one: when only the ruler remembers, she owns history. The fix is the most ordinary institution there is.

**The Isle remembers:**
- EP27's humans in their sky-proof valley (slate and turf, barns, the watchtower, the forest road, the sheep);
- EP28's Fenn in their open hall on the lake, speaking in light;
- the cairn on the meadow;
- the Grimmaw grazing the north-east meadow.

EP29's court grows on the open east meadow, between the lake and the cairn.

**Nothing graphic:** nobody dies on screen. People grow old and fade out of the time-lapse, heirs "wait" by greying in line, and the consort is replaced by the next one standing in the same place.

## 11. How to produce EP30 ("Make a Monster That People Worship", Arc 6)

1. **Branch:** `claude/worldcraft-ep30` from `main` once EP27–EP29 are merged (or from `claude/worldcraft-ep29`; merge `origin/main` first, keeping both sides). Read `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/STYLE_S2.md` ("Make every episode its own") and this brief.
2. **Plan entry:**
   - Hook: "What if the creature everyone fears is also the source of their harvest?"
   - Core: a dangerous creature can also give warmth, protection, medicine, fertility or sacred meaning. When people depend on what they fear, society gets more complicated.
   - CTA: "What creature would your civilization worship despite its danger?"
3. **Close EP29's loop:** EP29 ends "they pray to a monster", with a ring of candles lighting on the meadow right of the Queen's dais (`OFFER` in `make_ep29.py`) and the Queen looking up. EP30's frame 0 should show *what* they pray to.
   - The natural answer is the **Grimmaw**, the Isle's own monster since EP26, now with an altar.
   - Or a new creature whose body feeds the fields (its dung, its warmth, its shed scales).
4. **Give EP30 its own device** (not an editor, a point of view or a timeline). One idea: **a ledger / scale**. Every gift the monster gives (warmth, harvest, medicine) and every cost (fear, loss) lands on a balance beam over the world, and the society's rituals are what keep it level. Give it its own camera language (e.g. low and looking *up* at the creature, the worshipper's angle), motif colour (e.g. offering-candle gold or harvest green) and music band (e.g. a hymn / chant style, frame drums).
5. **Reuse:**
   - the Grimmaw (`predators`, `roar`, `alert`);
   - `people` for worshippers and harvesters (`royal` can become priest robes with a new colour);
   - `offerings` for the shrine;
   - `settlements` for farms;
   - the `graze` / `forest` layers for harvests.
6. **Pipeline notes:**
   - Kokoro needs Python ≤ 3.12, and `tts.py` needs `VIRTUAL_ENV` set to the venv (misaki installs its spaCy model with `uv pip`).
   - Regressions: fix `narration.wav` + `timing.json` and compare `score.py` + `mix.py` between this branch and the previous one (a `git worktree` of it with `node_modules` symlinked).
   - Remotion bundles `public/` when a render starts. If the audio changes during a render, re-mux the master's audio from the new `mix.wav` (as EP29 did) or render again.
   - With a long lens, plan where in-world teases land with a projection check (the camera maths in `src/s2/world/Stage.tsx` → `camPose`); EP29's first candle ring sat behind the crowd and under the next-run card.
   - Mind `captionsOff`: its `to` word is *exclusive*.
7. **Then** the same delivery: stills, a `--gl=swangle` render (about 18–20 min with the crowd), `qa.py`, encodes, SRT, storyboard, cover, this 11-section brief, docs, a new preview page with the Download button, push.
