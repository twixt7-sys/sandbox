# EP30 production brief: Make a Monster That People Worship

**Series:** Worldcraft Shorts, Episode 30 / 50. Season 2, "The World Engine", **Arc 6: Creatures and Species** (the arc's last episode).

**Plan entry** (`docs/SERIES_PLAN.md`):
- Hook: "What if the creature everyone fears is also the source of their harvest?"
- Topic: monsters as cultural and economic forces.
- Core: a dangerous creature can also provide warmth, protection, medicine, fertility or sacred meaning. When people depend on what they fear, the result is a more complicated society.
- CTA (a comment CTA): "What creature would your civilization worship despite its danger?"

**Deliverable:** a 60.2 s vertical Short (1080×1920, 30 fps) for YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Facebook Reels, plus Instagram Reels captions. The AI disclosure is on everywhere.

**Shot list and timings:** [`STORYBOARD.md`](STORYBOARD.md). The script that writes `episode.json`, including the whole world timeline, is `make_ep30.py`.

## The brief

The request: produce EP30 following EP26–EP29's patterns, style and vibe; close EP29's open loop ("they pray to a monster"); follow EP29's §11 hand-off; keep it about culture, economy and ordinary people's choices, with no violence on screen and no real-world religion depicted or mocked; end on the ending formula with a tease for EP31, which opens Arc 7 ("Your World Needs a History Nobody Agrees On"). The standing direction holds: each episode must be **its own** (`docs/STYLE_S2.md` → "Make every episode its own").

### EP30's own idea: the ledger

A **balance beam under the world**. GIFTS hang on the left pan, COSTS on the right, and the beam tips toward whatever weighs more, reading TIPPED, LEVEL, WORTH IT or EMPTY. Each ritual the village invents is a weight that moves the beam. The episode's argument is in the device: *worship is a deal with danger, and customs are how a society keeps the deal level.* The weights are read from the same keys as the world (the tithe on the ledger is the tithe at the altar), so the beam always matches the picture.

The ledger, beat by beat:
- **Lazy version:** HARVEST (1) against FEAR (2) and RAIDS (2): TIPPED. They drive it off.
- **Run 1:** "No more lost sheep": the costs lift off and the beam swings to WORTH IT; then the flocks eat the fields, HARVEST falls off too: **EMPTY**.
- **Tithe:** RAIDS lift off, one TITHE sheep hangs instead.
- **Holy ground:** FEAR halves.
- **Harvest:** HARVEST doubles: **LEVEL**.
- **Medicine:** MEDICINE joins the gifts: **WORTH IT**.
- **The twist (the keepers):** "it's always hungry": the tithe climbs to five sheep and the beam tips back: **TIPPED**.
- **The open rite:** the tithe falls back to one: **WORTH IT**.

**What else is EP30's own:**
- **The monster is the Isle's own.** The Grimmaw (EP26) is the worshipped creature. EP26 showed that deleting the predator lets the grazers strip the meadow; EP30 turns that ecology into an economy: the village's harvest depends on the thing it fears.
- **Camera language: the worshipper's angle.** Low and long-lensed, the monster against the sky with its people small below it (the hook: az −12, el 7, dist 40, fov 15). EP26 orbited, EP27 rode a dragon, EP28 sat at eye height, EP29 was a locked-off tripod.
- **Motif colour:** shrine madder `#ef6f9c` (the ledger's frame, the shrine's bands, the Grimmaw's painted stripes, the keepers' robes in a darker `#b84a72`, ONE SCALE, THE KEEPERS, FEARED & FED). Saffron was ruled out on purpose: robes in it would evoke a real tradition.
- **Music band** around the series theme:
  - `hymn`: choir aahs, a cello drone, a deep frame drum on the downbeat and the theme's first bars on voice oohs (the hook, and the keepers' twist);
  - `rite`: a kalimba ostinato, frame drums, a drone bass and the theme on pan flute, gaining one family per ritual;
  - the tempo is 92 BPM.
- **Sound:** `frameDrum` (a ritual moment) and `beamTip` (the ledger's beam shifting).
- **Fresh wording for the recurring beats:**
  - the drop: "No monster. No harvest.", stamped 0 HARVEST · NO MONSTER · NO FIELDS;
  - the thesis: FEAR AND NEED / ONE SCALE;
  - the checklist: GRIMMAW · THE DEAL / KEEP IT LEVEL;
  - run 2's verdict: FEARED & FED · 10 HARVESTS · 0 RAIDS;
  - the twist: RULE 5? THE KEEPERS, then IT'S ALWAYS / HUNGRY, then THE OPEN RITE · EVERYONE WATCHES.

**Why these rituals and this twist:** the plan lists warmth, protection, medicine, fertility and sacred meaning. On screen:
- protection and fertility as **harvest** (it keeps grazers off the crops: EP26's ecology);
- **medicine** (shed plates);
- sacred meaning as **holy ground** and the shrine itself;
- the cost made predictable as the **tithe**.

Warmth is left to the description and the comments. The keepers are the twist because they're the least obvious consequence of depending on what you fear: whoever mediates the fear gains power, and fear becomes something they can sell. The fix is an ordinary civic one, not a theological one: do it in the open.

**The Isle remembers:**
- EP27's humans in their sky-proof valley (slate and turf, barns, the watchtower, the road, the sheep);
- EP28's Fenn in their open hall on the lake;
- the cairn on the meadow;
- EP29's Undying Queen on her dais (she looks up with the Grimmaw at the end).

EP30's shrine stands on the east meadow before the Grimmaw; its fields are sown on the north meadow, outside its holy ground.

**Nothing graphic, no real religion:** the Grimmaw is driven off by walking away; sheep stand at the altar and are never shown taken; the rite is invented (a candle ring, sheaves, a wheat garland, painted stones, hooded keepers in madder) and resembles no real tradition's symbols or dress.

## 1. Research note

No new research was needed (`docs/SEASON2_RESEARCH.md`: the first-second hook, re-hooks every 10–15 s, the follow ask after the payoff, part numbers, the sound logo). The craft point is the plan's own: a worshipped monster is usually written as superstition, and the episode shows it as an economy instead, with the customs that grow round any dependency (tithes, sacred land, first fruits, specialists) and the classic failure of specialists who control access. The CTA invites invention ("what creature would *your* civilization worship"), which tends to produce long, specific comments.

## 7. Reusable components added

All are documented in `README.md` (Season 2 building blocks) and `docs/STYLE_S2.md`.
- **`ledger`:** any trade-off a society keeps in balance (Arc 9's economies, Arc 8's wars: what a border costs against what it protects). Items can follow world series, so the beam is always honest.
- **`shrine`:** a monument that rises, collects offerings and can be painted; `paint` is already EP31's opener (two tellings of one story).
- **`fields`:** farmland that ripens, fails or is grazed bare: any episode about food, famine or land.
- **`people.robe` and `people.gather`:** a class of specialists in their own dress (priests, guilds, soldiers) and a crowd that assembles at a place (a market, a trial, a rite, a battle line).
- **Predator `adorn`:** a creature marked by people (tamed, honoured, branded).
- **Music:** `hymn` (awe, reverence, fear) and `rite` (village life, ritual). **SFX:** `frameDrum`, `beamTip`.

## 11. How to produce EP31 ("Your World Needs a History Nobody Agrees On", Arc 7 opens)

1. **Branch:** `claude/worldcraft-ep31` from `main` once EP27–EP30 are merged (or from `claude/worldcraft-ep30`; merge `origin/main` first, keeping both sides). Read `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/STYLE_S2.md` ("Make every episode its own", and "Low angles on the Isle") and this brief.
2. **Plan entry:**
   - Hook: "What if the greatest hero in history was actually the worst person who ever lived?"
   - Core: history is interpreted through incomplete records, political interests, cultural values and personal memories. Conflicting accounts create mysteries that can drive entire stories.
   - CTA: "Follow for more ways to turn history into plot." (a follow CTA: it can merge with the ending formula, so pick a share or comment ask for the end card).
3. **Close EP30's loop:** EP30 ends "hero, or monster?" with a gold hero painted on one of the Grimmaw shrine's stones and a rust beast on the other (`paint` on the shrine in `make_ep30.py`), and the Grimmaw and the Queen looking up. EP31's frame 0 should show *the disputed figure*: the same event told two ways.
   - One reading: the Isle's two villages (EP27's humans, EP29's court) each raise a monument to the same person, one as a hero and one as a monster.
   - Or the Grimmaw itself: the villagers who drove it off (EP30's lazy version) remember a monster, the ones who worshipped it a god.
4. **Give EP31 its own device** (not an editor, a point of view, a timeline or a balance). One idea: **two chronicles side by side**, a split frame where the same run plays twice under two narrators, and the facts that agree glow while the ones that differ flicker. Or *strata*: the record as layers of the island's cut side (the "from the ground up" soil strata), each layer a source that later ones overwrite. Give it its own camera language (e.g. a slow vertical crane through the strata, or a split screen), a motif colour (e.g. archival sepia or ink blue) and a music band (e.g. a chronicle: lute, recorder; two keys at once for two accounts).
5. **Reuse:**
   - `people` + `lineage` for generations remembering differently (EP29's brief already pointed Arc 7 here);
   - the `library` (EP29) as a record that can be burnt or rewritten;
   - the shrine's `paint` and `rise` for monuments;
   - the `ledger` for weighing sources;
   - the Queen as the one witness who remembers (EP29's twist).
6. **Pipeline notes:**
   - Kokoro needs Python ≤ 3.12, and `tts.py` needs `VIRTUAL_ENV` set to the venv.
   - Regressions: fix `narration.wav` + `timing.json` and compare `score.py` + `mix.py` between this branch and the previous one; render stills from a `git worktree` of the previous branch with `node_modules` and `public` symlinked.
   - **Plan low cameras with a probe before rendering** (EP30 lost several rounds to it): `camPose` and the island's `heightAt` / `trees`, bundled with esbuild, give the line of sight; `project` (three.js) places landmarks in the frame. The Fenn's great hall on the lake and the cairn fill any frame they're in.
   - Remotion bundles `public/` when a render starts. If the audio changes during a render, re-mux the master's audio from the new `mix.wav` (EP29 and EP30 both did).
   - Mind `captionsOff`: its `to` word is *exclusive*, and it has no `nth` (a repeated word matches its first occurrence).
7. **Then** the same delivery: stills, a `--gl=swangle` render (about 20 min), `qa.py`, encodes, SRT, storyboard, cover, this 11-section brief, docs, a new preview page with the Download button, push.
