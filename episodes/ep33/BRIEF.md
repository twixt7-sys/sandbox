# EP33 production brief: How Do You Invent a Believable Religion?

**Series:** Worldcraft Shorts, Episode 33 / 50. Season 2, "The World Engine", **Arc 7: History and Mythology** (the arc's third episode). Built to EP31's reference standard and EP32's hand-off (§11 of its brief): start from `make_ep32.py`'s structure, the 25-row scorecard, the camera probe, beat stills before the render.

**Plan entry** (`docs/SERIES_PLAN.md`):
- Hook: "Don't start by inventing gods. Start by asking what people fear."
- Topic: religion and belief systems.
- Core: beliefs help people understand death, suffering, nature, morality and uncertainty. Rituals and institutions emerge around what a society considers sacred.
- CTA: "What question would the people of your world ask their gods?" (12 words). The scorecard caps the CTA at 10, so the video says "What question would your people ask their gods?" (8 words, the same ask); the descriptions and pinned comments keep the plan's full wording.

**Deliverable:** a 56.2 s vertical Short (1080×1920, 30 fps) for YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Facebook Reels, plus Instagram Reels captions. The AI disclosure is on everywhere.

**Shot list and timings:** [`STORYBOARD.md`](STORYBOARD.md). The script that writes `episode.json`, including the whole world timeline, is `make_ep33.py`.

## The brief

The request: make EP33 to the standard of EP31 and EP32; close EP32's loop (on "a god built from fear" the sun went dark over the Isle for a breath); give EP33 its own device, camera language, motif colour and music band, none used before; don't re-skin EP30 (worship of a real monster with a shrine and a ledger); tease EP34 ("What If Your World's Creation Myth Is Wrong?") with the ending formula; **no real-world religion** depicted, named or imitated, no violence; free tools before Figma; spend nothing.

### EP33's own idea: the fear map
**What a people fears is drawn on the Isle as shadows, and every belief is a light that answers one.** Seen from above, each fear is a creeping indigo pool over a place (death over the cairn, the lake, hunger over the field, the eclipse over the valley). A belief opens a lamplit pool inside its shadow, with a bright rim, a lamp post, and its rite growing in the light. The episode's argument is in the device: *a god is believable when you can point to the fear it answers.* Gods invented first are lamps standing in the sun: they answer nothing, so nobody comes, and they go out.

The fear map, beat by beat:
- **Frame 0 (the faith today, year 500):** the eclipse's round shadow lies over the valley and drifts east; inside it the crowd holds up lamps and looks at the sky; the four answered fears glow round the map (candles at the cairn, a ring of boats with lamps on the lake, sheaves round the field, the keepers' lamp in the valley). Tag: THE ECLIPSE · THE LAMPS STAY LIT.
- **The lazy version:** an unbelieving Isle in daylight, no shadows drawn at all (nobody asked what anyone fears). On "twelve" twelve gods' lamps pop up on sunny ground all over the map: LAMPS 12. Run 1 (300 years): three shrines rise, nobody walks to them, and the twelve lamps go out one by one: LAMPS 00, FAITHFUL 000.
- **The drop:** gloom: UNNEEDED · 12 GODS · 0 FEARS ANSWERED. "Nobody prays to a god they don't need."
- **The rewind** clears the shrines and lamps. On "dark" four shadows bloom over the map one after another, each tagged in dread grey: DEATH, THE LAKE, HUNGER, THE ECLIPSE.
- **Rules 1–4:** the map slides to each fear as it is named and pushes in as its answer is spoken; the light opens and pushes the shadow back to a ring: candles round the cairn (death), nine boats with lamps (the lake, nature), the field ripening and eight sheaves stood round it, shared (hunger: suffering, and a moral rule, *hoarding is a sin*), robed keepers among the crowd, lamps in people's hands and the tower bell (the eclipse: uncertainty, and an institution). LAMPS 01–04, FAITHFUL 040–220.
- **Run 2 (500 years):** an eclipse's shadow sweeps the whole Isle and every lamp holds inside it: STILL BURNING · 500 YEARS · 4 LAMPS · 480 FAITHFUL.
- **The twist:** RULE 5? THE DEEPEST FEAR. One shadow spreads over the entire map until only the four lamps burn: NOBODY / LISTENING?. On "listens" every face on the Isle turns up to the camera, and a light opens from the middle of the map and sweeps out past the coast: SO THEY MADE / A GOD. LAMPS 05.
- **"Write it last, and it feels real."** Row 5 unlocks: THE GOD · written last.

The four rules cover the plan's list: **death**, **nature** (the lake), **suffering** (hunger) with **morality** inside it (hoarding becomes a sin), and **uncertainty** (the eclipse) with an **institution** (keepers who read the sky). *Rituals and institutions emerge around what is sacred* is what the map shows: every rite and the keepers appear only inside a light.

**Why "nobody is listening".** The hook asks "What do people fear?". Rules 1–4 answer with four fears, and the twist names the one under all of them: that nobody is listening. That is what a god answers, so the god comes last, which pays off the hook's "Don't start with gods". The fair-play clue is planted at 5 s on the field card: the Lamplight faith "prays to an empty sky". The camera has been looking down from that sky all along, so when every face turns up at the twist, it turns to the viewer. It also pays off EP32's tease word for word: *a god built from fear*.

**Not a re-skin of EP30.** EP30 was one real monster, worshipped: a shrine, a ledger of what the beast gives and costs, the camera looking up at it. EP33 has no monster and no single shrine; its shrines are the *failure* (the lazy pantheon nobody visits), its subject is many fears and the beliefs that answer them, it is seen from straight above, and its god never appears as a figure at all: it is a light.

**What else is EP33's own:**
- **The camera language: the map table.** Every pose looks almost straight down at the Isle (el 80, az 0: north always up) and only slides and zooms across it, like a hand moving over a map on a table: a slide to each fear as it is named, a push in as its light opens, a pull out for each run. The only turn in the episode is the lesson, when the map rotates (3°/s). (EP26 orbited, EP27 rode a dragon, EP28 sat at eye height, EP29 was a locked-off tripod, EP30 looked up, EP31 split the frame, EP32 craned up and down the cut.)
- **Motif colour:** lamplight `#ffe45c` (the pools and their rims, the lamp posts, the lamps in people's hands and on the boats, the map tags once answered, LAMPS, the STILL BURNING stamp, A GOD). Its opposite is the fear map's dread grey `#b9b4d8` over indigo `#2b2766`.
- **Music band** around the series theme, at 96 BPM:
  - `vigil`: a night vigil (a tubular bell tolling the theme's first notes, D A D F, one per bar, over a bowed-glass pad, a cello drone and a sparse rain of celesta; no choir and no organ, so nothing sounds like a real liturgy): the hook, the dark map, and the moment they make a god;
  - `lantern`: a procession of lamps, one family per belief lit (a celesta ostinato; a hammered dulcimer; a hand drum and pizzicato bass; the theme on ocarina with bells on the roots).
- **Sound:** `lampOn` (a light opens), `creep` (shadows spread), `rift` (EP34's tease).
- **Fresh wording for the recurring beats:** the drop "Nobody prays to a god they don't need.", stamped UNNEEDED · 12 GODS · 0 FEARS ANSWERED; the thesis REAL FAITH / STARTS IN THE DARK; the checklist THE LAMPLIGHT · WHAT THEY FEAR / LIGHT THE DARK; run 2's verdict STILL BURNING; the twist RULE 5? THE DEEPEST FEAR, NOBODY / LISTENING?, SO THEY MADE / A GOD.

## 3. Narration (161 words, 56.24 s total)

> Inventing a religion? Don't start with gods. What do people fear? Meet the Lamplight faith. They pray to an empty sky. The lazy version: twelve gods first, one job each. Press run. Shrines rise. Nobody comes. The lamps go out. Nobody prays to a god they don't need. Real faith starts in the dark. Four fears come first. The fifth is the twist. One: death. Candles keep the dead company at the cairn. Two: the lake. No boat leaves without a lamp. Three: hunger. Hoarding grain becomes a sin. Four: the eclipse. Keepers rise to read the sky. Run it again. Five hundred years. Every lamp still burns. Rule five? The deepest fear. That nobody is listening. So they made someone who listens. A god. Write it last, and it feels real. Gods don't come first. Every god answers a fear. What question would your people ask their gods? Part 33 of 50. Follow for part 34: the gods are lying.

**How it is written** (the rules in `docs/STYLE_S2.md`, measured in §6):
- **The hook** is the plan's two imperatives turned into a question the twist can answer: "Inventing a religion? Don't start with gods. What do people fear?" (11 words).
- **"They pray to an empty sky."** The planted clue: the twist fills that sky.
- **The lazy version is the reader's default** (a pantheon, "twelve gods first, one job each"), and it fails for a story reason: "Nobody prays to a god they don't need."
- **Every rule has the same shape**, "N: fear." then one sentence of nine words or fewer that names the belief and the rite; rules 3 and 4 smuggle in morality (a sin) and an institution (keepers).
- **The twist answers the hook** and gives the writer one job: "Write it last, and it feels real."
- **The lesson** is a contrast that can be quoted: "Gods don't come first. Every god answers a fear."
- **The ask** is the plan's CTA at 8 words; the end card's starters (WHY DO WE DIE?, ARE YOU THERE?) lower the effort of a first reply, and the second one echoes the twist.

  - seen from above, people are small: the crowd's lamps and upturned faces read as points of light rather than faces, so the twist's "every face turns to the camera" is carried by the narration and the light more than by the faces;
  - at the hook the golden low sun throws long shadows of its own, so the eclipse's shadow reads as a dark band round the valley's lamplit pool rather than a crisp disc;
  - the map tags' type (20 px kicker, 28–30 px text) is at the series' phone minimum;
  - EP28's Fenn great hall is absent for this one episode.

## 7. Reusable components added

All are documented in `README.md` (Season 2 building blocks) and `docs/STYLE_S2.md` (motion vocabulary).
- **`world.fears`:** anything a people feels about a place, drawn on the map: fears, taboos, plague zones, a border nobody crosses, the reach of a law or a lord, light and shadow in a war of beliefs (Arc 8). `lit` is any answer that pushes a dread back; `umbra` is any moving shadow (an eclipse, a storm, an army); `rift` is anything breaking through the ground (EP34's buried truth).
- **`fearTags`:** map labels that change with the world's state.
- **`people.look`, `people.lamps`:** a crowd looking up at the sky (an omen, a dragon, a comet) and a crowd carrying lights (a vigil, a night march, a festival).
- **`lanterns`, `sheaves`:** boats with lamps on any water; a harvest laid out.
- **Music:** `vigil` (waiting in the dark) and `lantern` (a procession, something growing light by light). **SFX:** `lampOn`, `creep`, `rift`.

## 11. How to produce EP34 ("What If Your World's Creation Myth Is Wrong?")

1. **Branch:** `claude/worldcraft-ep34` from `claude/worldcraft-ep33` (merge `origin/main` first, keeping both sides). Read `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/STYLE_S2.md` (the reference-episode note, the writing rules, "Make every episode its own" including EP33's answer, "Making the next episode"), `docs/SEASON2_RESEARCH.md` §7, `episodes/ep31/BRIEF.md`, `episodes/ep32/BRIEF.md` and this brief.
2. **Plan entry:**
   - Hook: "Every child learns that the gods created the world. What if the gods are lying?"
   - Core: a creation myth can be culturally meaningful without being historically accurate. The gap between what people believe and what actually happened creates room for discovery and conflict.
   - CTA: "Would you reveal the truth to a civilization built on a lie?" (12 words: cut to 10 or fewer for the scorecard, e.g. "Would you tell them the truth?", and keep the full line in the descriptions).
3. **Close EP33's loop.** EP33 ends on "the gods are lying": on "lying" a crack of light opens in the meadow south of the lake for a breath (`fears` entry `rift`, from (−0.5, 10.6) to (12, 8.8)), the Queen looks up, and NEXT RUN · 34 THE LIE decodes from a sprite of the ground split by light. EP34's frame 0 could open that crack for good: what is under the Isle that the Lamplight's myth leaves out.
4. **Give EP34 its own device** (not an editor, a point of view, a timeline, a balance, chronicles, strata or a fear map). The strata (EP32) can hold the buried truth only as a callback. One idea: **the two skies**, the myth told as a painted dome over the Isle and the truth as what the ground shows, with each discovery tearing a hole in the painted sky. Pick a motif colour not yet used (not rust, ink, madder, teal, violet, verdigris or lamplight), a camera language not yet used (not orbit, ride, eye height, tripod, looking up, split frame, crane or overhead map), and a music band.
5. **No real-world religion or creation myth** is depicted, named or imitated: the Lamplight's myth is invented (EP33 never told it, so EP34 can). Check every generated image for real symbols.
6. **Reuse:** `world.fears` (`rift` for the crack, `umbra`), `fearTags`, `people.look`, `people.lamps`, the keepers (`people.robe` + `royal`), `strata` as a callback.
7. **Writing:** start from `make_ep33.py`'s structure, not its words. Measure with the real TTS (EP33's first draft started run 1 at 12.6 s; its second put the lesson at 44.9 s), then `python pipeline/benchmark.py ep34 ep33 ep32` and fix every ✗ before rendering a still.
8. **Pipeline notes:**
   - **Colours on the ground are blended in linear space:** a near-black tint over bright grass comes out olive, not dark. EP33's first shadows (`#15122e`) looked like dark grass; a lighter, bluer indigo (`#2b2766`) at a stronger blend reads as shadow. Check a tint on a still, not by the numbers.
   - **Overhead poses:** at el 80 a subject reads only at dist ≥ 100; the checklist card covers y 448–~840, so frame what matters in the lower half (the probe says where). Emissive meshes (lamp heads, the rift) are what shine from above; a cap on a lamp hides its light.
   - Plan every pose with `pipeline/camprobe.ts`, and render beat stills before trusting it.
   - The first still of a session can differ run to run, in either tree; render a throwaway first.
   - `loop_span()` and `tcue()` in `make_ep33.py` read the measured timing; re-run `make_epNN.py` after every TTS change.
   - Regression baselines first (`public/` is empty in a fresh container: `pipeline/build.sh ep25 ep32 ep33` on the branch, then save them).
9. **Then** the same delivery: stills, a `--gl=swangle` render, `qa.py`, encodes, SRT, storyboard, cover, this 11-section brief with the scorecard, docs, a new preview page with the Download button, push.
