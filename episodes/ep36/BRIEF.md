# EP36 production brief: Wars Need More Than Two Armies

**Series:** Worldcraft Shorts, Episode 36 / 50. Season 2, "The World Engine", **Arc 8: Conflict and Warfare** (the arc's first episode). Built to EP31's reference standard and EP35's hand-off (§11 of its brief): `make_ep35.py`'s structure, the 25-row scorecard, the camera probe, beat stills before the render.

**Plan entry** (`docs/SERIES_PLAN.md`):
- Hook: "Nobody sends a million soldiers to die just because a king is angry." (13 words; the scorecard caps the hook at 12, so the video says "Nobody sends a million soldiers to die for an angry king.", 11 words, ending at 3.47 s.)
- Core: wars emerge from competing interests, security fears, resources, ideology, succession disputes, alliances and miscalculation. Give each side a reason it considers worth the cost.
- CTA: "What would finally push your kingdom into war?" (8 words, spoken as written).

**Deliverable:** a 58.5 s vertical Short (1080×1920, 30 fps) for YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Facebook Reels, plus Instagram Reels captions. The AI disclosure is on everywhere.

**Shot list and timings:** [`STORYBOARD.md`](STORYBOARD.md). The script that writes `episode.json`, including the whole world timeline, is `make_ep36.py`.

## The brief

The request: make EP36 to the standard of EP31 and EP33–EP35; close EP35's loop (on "two armies are never enough" three small standards, red, blue and gold, were planted round the Builders' ruin, the Queen looked up, NEXT RUN · 36 THE WAR · MORE THAN TWO SIDES decoded, and they sank); give EP36 its own device, camera language, motif colour and music band, none used before; tease EP37 ("The Strongest Army Can Still Lose") with the ending formula; **no real-world war, army, flag, uniform, emblem, religion or nation** depicted, named or imitated, standards plain single colours, **no violence on screen**; free tools before Figma; spend nothing.

### EP36's own idea: the war room's threads
**A war is real when every side has a reason it would pay for, and it starts by mistake.** Three sides are camped round the Builders' ruin (EP35's wall) and the war hasn't started yet: the Ruin War. Every reason a side has for war is **a coloured thread** pinned from its camp to what it wants, and the war starts where the threads meet: **a knot on a tall war pin stuck in the ruin**, glowing flashpoint lime. Under the world sits **the war room**, a strip with one column per side: each reason is pinned in as its thread goes down (icon + a few words), and a verdict at the foot of each column reads WORTH IT? until the side has a reason worth the cost. The lazy version is the reader's default, two armies and one angry king: one thread (anger), and after ten years of shouting nobody marches; the camps are struck and the armies go home (NOT WORTH IT · NOBODY CAME). Each rule pins one thread, each a kind from the plan: **something to gain** (Red needs the ruin's harbour: interests and resources), **fear** (Blue can't let Red hold it: security), **a claim** (Gold's prince says it's his by birth: succession), **allies** (Blue swore to stand with Gold: a thread braided blue and gold). Run 2: ten years, three armies march up and stand facing one ruin: EVERYONE'S RIGHT. The locked fifth row is **miscalculation**: nobody here wants this war; each side is sure the others will blink (every thread pulls taut and hums, and every column adds "they'll blink"); all three are wrong (the knot catches with a lime flare and sparks, every guess is struck through: GUESSED WRONG, and the war room reads IT CATCHES). Nobody moves and nothing is shown fighting: the war starts off screen, in the knot. The writer's job: "So give every side a reason worth the cost." The line to keep: *Nobody thinks they're the villain.*

Beat by beat:
- **Frame 0 (year 10 of the standoff):** golden hour, looking south over the lake at the ruin: the knot on its pin against the amber glow, the blue thread and the red thread arcing down to the two armies standing at the wall, the braided ally thread. A MILLION SOLDIERS / FOR ONE ANGRY KING? is on screen; each line pulses on its word, and on "angry" a tag pins to the knot: NOT ONE KING · 3 SIDES · 4 REASONS.
- **The lazy version:** two camps, one red thread (anger) from Red to Blue; TWO ARMIES / ONE ANGRY KING; the war room slides in (NO WAR; Gold's column empty). Run 1 (year 0→10): 10 YEARS / OF SHOUTING / NOBODY MARCHES; on "Nobody" both camps strike their tents, the standards sink, the armies go home and the thread reels back (ARMIES 2→0). The drop: JUST A TANTRUM · 1 REASON · 0 ARMIES · NO WAR.
- **The rewind:** three camps, no threads (NO REASONS); REAL WARS / NEED REASONS; the checklist PULL THE THREADS with row 5 locked.
- **Rules 1–4,** each a cut close on one side's pin and a pull-back along its thread to the knot: on "harbour" the war pin is pushed into the ruin and Red's thread runs to it (RED · GAIN · A HARBOUR TO TAKE); on "Red" Blue's thread meets it (BLUE · FEAR · CAN'T LET RED WIN; 1 FLASHPOINT); on "prince" Gold's thread (GOLD · A CLAIM · HIS BY BIRTH); on "swore" the braided blue-and-gold thread (BLUE + GOLD · ALLIES · A PROMISE TO KEEP).
- **Run 2:** the whole Isle, year 0→10; the three armies advance from their camps and stand facing the ruin: 10 YEARS / THREE ARMIES / ONE RUIN; EVERYONE'S RIGHT · 3 SIDES · 4 REASONS · 1 RUIN.
- **The twist:** dusk, close on the knot: RULE 5? NOBODY WANTS IT; on "sure" every thread pulls taut (THEY'LL BLINK); on "wrong" the knot catches (ALL WRONG; THE FLASHPOINT · ONE WRONG GUESS), every "they'll blink" is struck through, GUESSED WRONG.
- **"So give every side a reason worth the cost."** Day returns, the threads slacken, row 5 unlocks: A GUESS · they'll back down · 5/5.

**Why the twist answers the hook.** The hook says nobody goes to war for an angry king, so why do wars happen? The rules give every side a reason it would pay for; the twist shows that even then nobody chooses the war: each side bets the others will back down, and the bet is what starts it. The fair-play clue is on screen from frame 0 (the knot already glowing where the threads meet) and on the card ("A WAR · NOT YET"; "three, all sure they're right").

**Not a re-skin of EP35.** EP35 read a lost people off what they left in the ground; EP36 is the living, on the same ground, each wanting it for a different reason. EP35's wall and finds are the contested ruin; its finds tray and repair log are not used.

**What else is EP36's own:**
- **The camera language: the pull-back.** Every beat starts close on one thing (a pin, a camp, the knot) and the camera backs away along its own line of sight (target fixed, the frame's half-height growing ×2.2–3.0) to reveal what that thing is tied to; a closer view is always a cut, never a push. The hook pulls back slowly while the title holds; each rule cuts close on a side's pin from a side-on angle (Red +60°, Blue −45°, Gold +60° off the line to the knot) and pulls back until the thread reaches the knot; the twist cuts close on the knot and pulls back further and further as it catches; the loop cuts under the wipe to a closer view and pulls back onto frame 0. (EP26 orbited, EP27 rode a dragon, EP28 sat at eye height, EP29 was a locked-off tripod, EP30 looked up, EP31 split the frame, EP32 craned, EP33 looked down at a map table, EP34 dolly-zoomed, EP35 tracked sideways.)
- **Motif colour:** flashpoint lime `#c8f04a` (the knot, the war room's frame and status, REASONS, the flashpoint ring, EVERYONE'S RIGHT, ALL WRONG). The sides keep EP35's plain red `#c0573c`, blue `#4f7fbf` and gold `#d4b23f`.
- **Music band** around the series theme, at 96 BPM:
  - `council`: the war room at night (a clock ticking on the side-stick, taut threads plucked as pizzicato fifths, harpsichord chords, the theme on a muted trumpet): the hook, the lazy version (`thin`) and the twist after "sure";
  - `muster`: the armies gathering, one family per rule (a field snare in rudiments; a tuba marching on root and fifth; timpani and a soft cymbal; the theme on a fife with horns). It is a march, never a battle.
- **Sound:** `pinIn` (a pin pushed into the map), `threadPull` (a thread drawn taut), `spark` (the flashpoint catching: a fizz into a soft thump, never an explosion).
- **Fresh wording for the recurring beats:** the drop "One angry king is a tantrum, not a war.", stamped JUST A TANTRUM · 1 REASON · 0 ARMIES · NO WAR; the thesis REAL WARS / NEED REASONS; the checklist THE RUIN WAR · WHY THEY MARCH / PULL THE THREADS; run 2's verdict EVERYONE'S RIGHT; the twist RULE 5? NOBODY WANTS IT, THEY'LL BLINK, ALL WRONG, THE FLASHPOINT.

## 7. Reusable components added

All are documented in `README.md` (Season 2 building blocks) and `docs/STYLE_S2.md` (motion vocabulary).
- **`thread` / `knot`:** any web of causes or obligations on the map: alliances before a war (Arc 8), trade routes and debts, a succession's claims, a conspiracy board, a prophecy's strands meeting at one place.
- **`camp` + `people.sideShow` / `advance`:** armies gathering, going home or marching up to stand (EP37's logistics: a camp that can't be fed thins out).
- **`warRoom`:** any per-side tally with reasons and a verdict: a council's votes, a trial's arguments, a negotiation's demands.
- **The pull-back:** `back()` in `make_ep36.py` for any reveal from a detail to the whole.
- **Music:** `council` (deliberation, tension, a ticking clock) and `muster` (a gathering, a procession, an army; `layer` 1–4). **SFX:** `pinIn`, `threadPull`, `spark`.
